Billing Pattern Analysis

Your collection rate can be excellent while your practice loses money

A 95% collection rate means you collected 95% of what you were owed. It is a real metric and worth tracking.

It also cannot see two of the four largest revenue leaks in a therapy practice.

Downcoding. You billed the extended session. The payer paid the standard rate. That is not a denial — the claim processes, the money arrives, and nothing flags it. Your allowed amount just got smaller, and you collected 100% of the smaller number. Your collection rate goes up.

Sessions that never became claims. A completed session sitting in your EHR that was never submitted is in neither the numerator nor the denominator. It never entered the math at all.

Both are invisible in a deposit-level view. Both compound month over month. Neither shows up in your P&L as anything other than revenue being lower than you expected.

What the analysis covers

Code mix and concentration

What share of your sessions is each CPT code, and is any one code carrying more weight than your documentation supports? A 90837 rate above 50% of billed sessions is a flag. A flat distribution — the same code on nearly every encounter — is a different flag, and usually means the code has become a default rather than a reflection of time.

Billed versus paid, at the code level

Comparing what you submitted against what the ERA actually paid, code by code. Occasional downcodes are normal. A pattern is a documentation-to-billing gap, and it is quantifiable.

Payer-level performance

Denial rate, days to payment, and allowed amounts measured against expected rates — by payer, not in aggregate. One slow payer or one reimbursing below contract can drag an otherwise healthy practice.

Revenue separation

Individual, family, and group psychotherapy are separate reimbursement buckets. When 90846, 90847, and 90853 collapse into a single "therapy income" line, a denial pattern affecting only family sessions is impossible to see.

Telehealth reconciliation

POS 10 and POS 02 reimburse differently for the same session. If your export doesn't separate them, your telehealth revenue analysis is wrong.

Session-to-claim reconciliation

Completed visits compared against submitted claims for the same period. A full schedule is not the same as clean revenue.

What I need from you

A claims export covering the last 6 to 12 months, including: CPT code, date of service, payer, billed amount, allowed amount, paid amount, and adjustment. Denial and adjustment reason codes where your system provides them.

Appointment or session data for the same period, so scheduled volume can be reconciled against billed volume.

Most practices on TheraNest, SimplePractice, or TherapyNotes can produce this. If yours can't, I'll tell you before you pay rather than after.

Turnaround is one week from receiving usable data.

Where my work ends

This is financial analysis. It is not coding advice, not a documentation audit, and not a compliance review.

I identify patterns in the money and quantify what they are worth. Specific coding decisions belong with a certified coder, documentation questions belong with your clinical team, and claims corrections belong with your biller. The report tells you what to bring them.

That boundary is deliberate and it appears in every deliverable.

Who this is for

Therapy and mental health private practices that bill insurance. The analysis needs claim volume to produce meaningful patterns, so it works best for group practices with two or more clinicians. Established solo practitioners with consistent insurance volume also qualify.

If your practice is primarily private pay, this is not the right engagement — there are no claims to analyze. A standard financial review would serve you better.
